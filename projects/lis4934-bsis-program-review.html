USF LIS4934: BSIS Program Review

Published: 4/19/2026

Introduction

Throughout my Data Science & Analytics concentration at USF, I have taken a variety of courses that have strengthened my industry-applicable skills. While many of these focused on R, a programming language for statistical computing, and RStudio, an integrated development environment (IDE) designed for R, I’ve also taken courses and developed proficiency in other languages, such as Python, Java, and SQL. Furthermore, my minor in Studio Art provided me with a unique blend of skills in drawing, videography, and sculpting classes. In this essay, I will discuss how my most significant courses and projects have prepared me for a career in data science.

Notable Courses & Assignments

Courses

Four courses which I felt developed my greatest strengths were LIS4370 - R Programming for Data Science, LIS4800 – Introduction to Data Science, LIS2780 – Database Concepts, and ART3635 - Sel Topics in Film & Video.

LIS4370, R Programming for Data Science, teaches students how to apply R in practical data analytic scenarios. Assignments test students on their understanding of functions, data visualization, math, and debugging. I maintained my R source code on a GitHub repository, which the professor would check along with my blog. This was one of the more significant courses because it allowed me to practice GitHub version control and explain my code in “layman’s terms," enhancing my writing muscles and shifting my focus more to business values.

LIS4800, Introduction to Data Science, teaches students the fundamentals of the R programming language and R Studio, the IDE used for R. Many of the assignments consisted of coding exercises and completing DataCamp courses, a platform filled with R and Python data science tutorials. Quizzes were frequent and tested students on R fundamentals. This course was one of the more challenging and technical courses I’ve taken, despite being an “introduction” class. However, I learned a lot about R programming practices, functions, syntax, and packages.

LIS2780, Database Concepts, teaches database fundamentals, including Microsoft Access, SQL, and entity-relationship models. Students were taught concepts in normalization, primary keys, and foreign keys, and were given SQL exercises to practice this knowledge. Assignments focused on introducing the basics of SQL and entity relationships rather than building advanced skills. Although this wasn’t an upper-level class, it was influential purely because it was the only class in the Data Science concentration that taught SQL.

Additionally, ART3635, Selected Topics in Film & Video, while not a part of my major, was also a notable course. It teaches students skills in filmmaking, videography, and video editing in Adobe Premiere Pro. As an illustrator with little interest in live-action filming, I showed little interest in it at first, but after taking it, my perspective changed. Videography instilled in me a greater attentiveness towards minute details in my craft. It made me more disciplined with the level of my work, and it even strengthened my ability to draw a wider array of scenes.

Assignments

LIS4370 and LIS2780, which I listed as the most notable courses, similarly had the most impactful final projects; however, I also found the final project from LIS4204, Information Behaviors, equally helpful. These helped strengthen my knowledge and skills in object-oriented programming, database management, and online information behaviors, respectively.

For LIS4370’s final project, I created a package in R and demonstrated my ability to handle object-oriented classes and functions (R’s S3 classes), documentation, and GitHub. As I worked through this assignment, my skills in debugging and documentation were most challenged, as I had little experience with either.

For LIS2780’s final project, I created an imaginary relational database for a doctor's office. The project consisted of three phases: (1) developing an entity-relationship diagram for the office, (2) determining the relationship between tables and variables, and (3) creating the database in Microsoft Access and running queries on sample data. This assignment challenged my skills in database architecture and SQL code organization. However, after finishing this project, I mastered the skill of building relationships between tables in a real-world scenario.

Lastly, for LIS4204’s final project, I analyzed and critiqued six peer-reviewed articles relating to information behaviors on online platforms in a video presentation. Through this project, I learned to interpret various theoretical models and research methodologies, as well as critically critique each author’s perspective.

These projects have improved my technical and soft skills. LIS4370 and LIS2780’s final project strengthened my R programming and SQL skills, respectively – both of which are popular programming languages in the industry – and have taught me endurance and resilience, which is a crucial skill for completing lengthy projects. LIS4204’s final projects helped me develop critical analysis skills, which will aid me when I need to prove the existence of a problem and the need for an algorithmic solution.

Conclusion

My time as a student was well spent on practical courses, which, overall, will help prepare me as I transition to industry. I’ve learned valuable skills in programming languages, such as R, Python, Java, and SQL, and soft skills in communication, documentation, and critical thinking. One critique I have, however, is the lack of collaboration and in-person classes. As the concentration was nearly remote, connecting with others was challenging, and I feel that in-person would have fostered more opportunities for networking and job-seeking. Overall, while the Data Science & Analytics concentration had its flaws, I feel that the rigor and intensity make up for it.
